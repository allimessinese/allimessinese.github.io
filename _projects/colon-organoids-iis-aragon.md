## Overview

During a research internship at the **Instituto de Investigación Sanitaria Aragón (IIS Aragón)** in Zaragoza, Spain, I worked on cutting-edge organ regeneration research. This experience gave me direct exposure to some of the most advanced techniques in regenerative medicine, including organoid culture and decellularization.

The lab was working on approaches to address the shortage of donor organs for transplantation - developing scaffolds and organoid models as potential building blocks for future transplantable tissues.

## My Contribution

- **Colon organoids:** Contributed to the isolation and development of colon organoids - 3D tissue models derived from patient cells that recapitulate the structure and function of the colon
- **Liver decellularization:** Monitored pig fetus liver decellularization - the process of removing all cells from an organ to leave behind an extracellular matrix scaffold that can be repopulated with patient-specific cells
- **Pioneering procedure:** Observed a transplantation procedure involving a **revascularized pig liver scaffold** - a landmark step toward building functional transplantable organs

## Why It Mattered

Organ regeneration sits at the frontier of biomedical science. Decellularized scaffolds and organoids represent two complementary strategies for eventually replacing diseased or damaged organs without the rejection risks of traditional transplantation.
