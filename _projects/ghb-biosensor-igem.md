## What I Learned

This was my first exposure to entrepreneurship and commercialisation, and it fundamentally changed how I think about research. Taking a scientific idea and pressure-testing it against real markets, regulatory frameworks, and funding realities, within the constraints of a student competition, compressed years of business learning into a few months.

It was also my first experience seeing a product travel the full arc from ideation to a functioning prototype. Watching the the BlcR-electrode system to produce a measurable capacitance signal, while simultaneously building the business case for bringing it to nightclubs, made the connection between bench science and real-world impact viscerally concrete.

The patent was a first too: understanding what is protectable, why timing matters, and what a priority filing actually secures was a crash course in IP strategy that has shaped how I approach every product-stage project since.

## Overview

Drink spiking with GHB (gamma-hydroxybutyric acid) surged across Western Europe in 2021 and 2022. The drug is colourless, odourless, and metabolises within hours, making it almost impossible to prove after the fact. Existing detection products were either too slow, too expensive, or required active steps that no one takes in a busy venue.

Our team at TU Delft built **SPYKE**: a bioelectronic sensor integrated directly into a drinking cup that monitors for GHB passively and continuously, triggering an orange LED warning the moment it detects the drug.

At the **2022 iGEM Giant Jamboree in Paris**, SPYKE was awarded **2nd place for Best Entrepreneurship** and reached the finals in the **Diagnostics track**.

## The Science

The detection mechanism exploits a natural bacterial protein called **BlcR**, from *Agrobacterium tumefaciens*. BlcR normally binds to DNA operator sequences to regulate metabolic genes. The key property: when GHB binds to BlcR, the protein releases from the DNA.

We engineered a sensor around this behaviour:

1. BlcR proteins are tethered via DNA oligonucleotides to a **gold interdigitated electrode** embedded in the cup
2. When GHB enters the drink (via a membrane separating the sensor compartment from the liquid), it binds BlcR and causes the protein to release from the electrode
3. The release changes the electrode's **capacitance**, a measurable electrical shift
4. Circuitry detects the threshold change and activates an LED warning

The team validated BlcR immobilisation with atomic force microscopy and demonstrated measurable capacitance changes when GHB analogues were introduced. Five measurement techniques were used across the validation process: fluorescence assays, isothermal titration calorimetry, electrophoresis mobility shift assays, atomic force microscopy, and electrical measurements.

## My Role: CFO and Entrepreneurship Lead

My work sat at the intersection of commercialisation, fundraising, and stakeholder strategy.

**Patent.** We filed a [priority patent](/patents/) on September 30, 2022, covering the detection method, protein and DNA sequences, hardware design, and the GHB biosensor application. The filing gave us a one-year window to develop the concept further before committing to an international application. It was the first patent I had been involved in: navigating what was protectable, what wasn't, and why the filing date mattered more than I had expected.

**Market and competitive analysis.** I led a full Porter's Five Forces and SWOT analysis. The competitive landscape was clear: no passive, continuous, in-situ detection system existed on the market. Current alternatives ranged from €3.52 to €24.90 per test, all requiring active use. SPYKE's cost structure came to roughly €2.92 for biological components and €61.53 for hardware per unit, with 50 to 100% margins once manufacturing scaled.

**Fundraising and partnerships.** I secured €100K+ in funding and built 10+ partnerships across biotech, pharma, and institutional sponsors, representing the team in sponsor negotiations, investor pitches, and stakeholder meetings.

**Human practices.** We ran a value-sensitive design process with hospitals, club owners, drug harm reduction organisations, and survivors of spiking. This shaped both the product design and the commercialisation roadmap, particularly around privacy, data use, and avoiding unintended consequences.

**Commercialisation roadmap.** The planned trajectory ran from R&D optimisation through legal registration and food safety certification (CE Marking, FDA, FCM, FCC) to a target market launch of Q1 2025, with manufacturing anchored in the Netherlands for regulatory and supply chain reasons.

## The Prototype

By the end of the competition, SPYKE existed as a functioning prototype: a cup with an integrated sensor compartment, electrode, and LED circuit that produced a real electrical signal in response to GHB. Going from whiteboard to working device in a single competition cycle, with a student team, no prior product development experience, and a tight deadline, was the formative experience of my undergraduate years.
