## Overview

My bachelor thesis at TU Delft focused on understanding how a specific protein regulates **lipid biosynthesis in *Bacillus subtilis*** — a model gram-positive bacterium widely studied for both fundamental biology and biotechnology applications.

Lipid biosynthesis is a fundamental cellular process, and its regulation has implications for both basic science (understanding bacterial physiology) and applied research (engineering bacteria for lipid-derived products).

## My Contribution

- Conducted in-depth experiments using **cloning** and **fluorescence microscopy** to investigate the regulatory role of a key protein in lipid biosynthesis
- Applied molecular biology techniques to elucidate the protein's influence on lipid biosynthesis mechanisms
- Collaborated with lab members to **optimize experimental protocols**, achieving a **15% increase in data accuracy** for fluorescence imaging and protein analysis
- Contributed to advancing knowledge in microbial lipid regulation

## Methods

- Molecular cloning (restriction-ligation, Gibson assembly)
- Fluorescence microscopy and image analysis
- Protein expression and purification
- Biochemical assays for lipid pathway activity

## Context

This thesis was completed as part of my BS in Nanobiology at TU Delft and ran concurrently with my Research Internship in the same lab (February–June 2024), where I extended this work further with additional cloning experiments and fluorescence microscopy on protein-lipid interactions.
